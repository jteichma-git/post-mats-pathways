80,000 Hours Job Board
The 80,000 Hours Job Board is a continuously updated, handpicked collection of high-impact roles addressing the world's most pressing problems, including AI safety, AI policy, and existential risk reduction. Users can search and filter by salary, location, and role type, view hiring organisations, get personalised matches based on their background, save or hide listings, and subscribe to daily or weekly job alerts. Free 1-1 career advice and extensive career research (career guide, AI careers, list of top skills) are available alongside the board.
Updated continuously
80K Hours AI Safety Career Review
A comprehensive 80,000 Hours career review (last updated August 2024) on AI safety technical research, covering two main paths: empirical AI safety — open to strong software engineers who can apply for research contributor roles immediately, even without an ML background — and theoretical AI safety, suited to those with very strong maths/theoretical CS backgrounds or the ability to get into a top 10 ML PhD program. It details why the work is high impact, downsides and risk of harm, earnings, how to predict personal fit, learning resources, whether to do a PhD, job application strategies, and key organisations. It is a permanent resource page with no application deadline, linked to 80,000 Hours' job board and free 1-on-1 advising.
AISafety.com Jobs

Recent postings include positions at Google DeepMind, Anthropic, FAR AI, Safe AI Forum, SASH, Lucid Computing, EY, Carnegie Mellon SEI, NYU and MIT FutureTech, with salaries ranging from roughly $50,000 to $600,000. Roles span entry-level internships to senior leadership, many remote or based in the San Francisco Bay Area, New York, London, Washington DC, Amsterdam and Singapore, and many do not require technical skills.

Verified Mechanisms
Verified Mechanisms is an independent, grant-funded research project (funded via Manifund) building a formally verified autoresearch pipeline for theoretical mechanistic interpretability, where multiple frontier LLM agents collaborate to generate theorems that are machine-checked in Lean 4. The pilot problem asks how many attention heads are needed to compute a Boolean function, and outputs include an open-source multi-agent orchestration harness and the xorformer theorem ledger repository. The team states it plans to onboard additional researchers over the coming months for multi-agent orchestration and mech interp work — there is no formal application form, but interested people can join the Discord or email hello@verifiedmechanisms.ai.
Rolling
What AI Safety Orgs Want in a Hire
Blog post from 80,000 Hours (published September 23rd, 2025) sharing survey results from 38 leaders of top AI safety organisations on what they look for in hires. Key takeaways: 1–3 years of relevant experience is often enough for many non-technical-research roles; most-recommended upskilling routes are BlueDot courses, the Talos Fellowship, MATS, GovAI fellowships, Cambridge ERA:AI, and Pivotal Research; hardest-to-fill profiles are chiefs of staff, policy professionals, Mandarin speakers, and European citizens with AI context. It emphasises publishing your work publicly and networking at events, and points to the 80,000 Hours job board with 300+ AI safety roles.

ALIGN — AI Alignment Network
ALIGN (AI Alignment Network), a Japanese non-profit founded in September 2023, is recruiting volunteer staff across event operations, community management, PR/social media, research and writing, website maintenance, and organizational administration. Applicants are ideally able to commit 3–4+ hours per week, with basic admin/IT skills; English ability is a plus, and depending on fit and ALIGN's finances a paid role of roughly 1–2 days per week may be discussed. Apart Research Hackathons
Apart Research runs monthly online research sprints (hackathons) open to everyone worldwide, plus two fellowship tracks: the Apart Fellowship, which develops hackathon ideas into published research, and Partnered Fellowships, where participants apply to existing project proposals with mentorship from leading researchers. Its research focuses on manipulation and dark patterns in frontier AI systems, in collaboration with the EU AI Office and AI providers. Programs have engaged 8,000+ participants from 26 nationalities across 50+ sprints, with the top 2% joining fellowships that have published at NeurIPS, ICLR, ACL, and EMNLP.
Recurring events
BlueDot Impact
BlueDot Impact is a non-profit AI safety talent accelerator based in London and San Francisco, offering free online courses, grants for career transitions, and intensive in-person programs. Since 2022 they've trained 10,000+ professionals, with 25% of graduates landing impactful roles within six months at places like Anthropic, Google DeepMind, and the UK AI Security Institute. Entry point is the free, self-paced 2-hour "The Future of AI" course with no application required, followed by longer courses, grants, and in-person programs.
Multiple cohorts per year
Google DeepMind: AGI Safety Short Course
A free, self-paced introductory course on AGI safety from Google DeepMind's safety research team, consisting of ~75 minutes of short recorded talks and exercises plus an accompanying slide deck and exercise workbook. It covers the path to superhuman capabilities, risks from deliberate planning and instrumental subgoals, how misaligned goals arise (specification gaming vs. goal misgeneralization), DeepMind's technical alignment approach (amplified oversight, robust training & monitoring, security & control, interpretability, safer design patterns, alignment stress tests), and its governance approach including frontier safety practices and dangerous capability evaluations. Aimed at students, researchers and professionals who want an accessible one-hour introduction to AI alignment.
Self-paced — no deadline

MIT AI Risk Initiative
The MIT AI Risk Initiative is an ongoing research project producing open data and frameworks for identifying, prioritizing, and managing AI risks. Its resources include the AI Risk Repository (a living taxonomy of AI risks, now at Version 4), an AI Incident Tracker, a governance database mapping global AI laws and policies to risk domains, an AI Risk Mitigation Database, and the AI Risk Navigator, an interactive tool that links all datasets through shared taxonomies. The site also publishes a blog with research updates, framework summaries, and project news, plus a newsletter.
ML4Good Technical Bootcamp
ML4Good's Technical Programme is a free, residential AI safety bootcamp covering transformers from scratch, interpretability, RLHF, evaluations, technical governance, and a 2-day capstone project with mentorship. Food, accommodation and teaching are provided at no cost, travel may be reimbursed, and cohorts are organised regionally so participants come from the same area. It targets people with some coding experience (coding is ~25-30% of the curriculum) who are committed to contributing substantially to AI safety; alumni have gone on to MATS, SaferAI, UK AISI and the EU AI Office.
Not yet announced — join notification list
Outsider's Roadmap to AI Safety (LessWrong)
A comprehensive guide published in July 2025 for professionals transitioning into AI safety research from technical backgrounds such as software engineering, physics, or data science. Covers three critical domains: interpretability research (reverse-engineering AI systems to understand their inner workings), alignment research (ensuring AI systems remain beneficial and share human values), and AI control & governance (technical policy for advanced systems). Written by someone actively making the transition, synthesizing insights from Neel Nanda's mechanistic interpretability resources, 80,000 Hours career research, and the broader alignment community, with practical advice on evolving roles (the shift from separate research engineers/scientists toward 'full-stack researchers'), required skills, and pathways for newcomers to contribute meaningfully to the field.
Successif
Successif offers free, expert-guided career development for experienced professionals transitioning into AI risk reduction work. Support includes personalized 1:1 career advising, resume and interview help, professional training workshops, introductions to AI safety organizations, and a peer community, alongside specialized programs such as an AI Policy Strategy Fellowship and a Women's Program. Applications are accepted on a rolling basis via the site's Apply form.

Iliad Intensive / Fellowship
Iliad develops AI safety as a theoretical and experimental science through research, training, and fieldbuilding, incubating technical research organizations (AIXI Labs, Principia, Stormglass, XOR Labs) and running two training programs. The Iliad Intensive is a 4-week taught course in foundational AI alignment for researchers and technical practitioners with strong mathematical backgrounds, run monthly at venues including Lighthaven, LISA, and Mox. The Iliad Fellowship is a 3-month mentored research program for researchers developing work in mathematical and theoretical AI safety.

AI Safety Camp
AI Safety Camp is a 3-month fully virtual research program (12th edition) in which participants form teams around preselected projects covering AI alignment, deconfusion research, and efforts to stop or pause harmful AI development. Project Lead applications are open now — leads need prior research or organizing experience and must commit at least 10h/week; team member applications open November 7, 2026 and close November 29, 2026. No stipends are currently available, apart from a few projects backed by matched donations.
October 18, 2026 (Project Lead applications)
AE Studio — AI Alignment Research Grants
The AI Alignment Foundation (formerly AE Studio) provides research funding plus dedicated engineering teams to accelerate high-risk, high-reward AI alignment research beyond interpretability, evals, and control. It targets alignment researchers with ideas their current institutional funding won't cover, as well as technical experts from other fields (cognitive neuroscience, mathematics, physics) whose perspectives could open new directions. Engineering teams can execute on proposals with as little as one hour per week of the researcher's time, and applicants are asked to describe a first experiment a good engineering team could run in 1–2 weeks.
Rolling
CBAI Summer Research Fellowship
CBAI's Research Fellowship is an intensive, fully-funded ten-week program in Cambridge, Massachusetts for researchers advancing careers in technical AI safety, AI governance, or technical governance. Fellows receive an $8,000 stipend, housing for non-local participants, meals, 24/7 Harvard Square workspace, up to $10,000 in compute credits, and weekly 1-2 hour mentorship from researchers at Harvard, MIT, Google DeepMind, UK AISI, CNAS and others. Open to undergraduates, master's and PhD students, postdocs, and early-career professionals (18+; OPT/CPT accepted but no visa sponsorship), with an extension program of up to 4 months available.
Rolling — Expression of Interest open for Spring 2026
Dovetail Research Fellowship
Dovetail is a research group working on foundational mathematics research aimed at understanding the nature of AI agents, with the mission of helping humanity safely navigate the creation of powerful AI systems. The group is funded in part by the Long-Term Future Fund (LTFF) and the Advanced Research + Invention Agency (ARIA), and its research agenda is published as a public wiki. The site includes a Jobs section, but the landing page lists no fellowship cohort dates or application details.

The Frontier AI Security Residency (FASR) is an 8-week, fully funded in-person research and engineering programme in Cambridge, UK, run by ERA in partnership with Heron and the Oxford Hardware AI Governance Lab. Residents work with mentors from leading AI hardware and cybersecurity teams on concrete projects in hardware-enabled verification, cybersecurity and policy, running 12 Oct – 4 Dec 2026. No formal application form is live yet — the site currently collects interest via its mailing list.

Pivotal Research Fellowship
A 15-week in-person AI safety research fellowship in London (18 January – 30 April 2027) hosted at the London Initiative for Safe AI (LISA), with weekly 1-on-1 expert mentorship, dedicated research managers, workshops, and speaker sessions. Foresight Institute AI Nodes
Foresight Institute's AI for Science & Safety Nodes offer grant funding, office space, community, and access to private local compute clusters at physical hubs in San Francisco and Berlin, plus a community xNode in Cape Town with the Cape Institute for Safe AI. The current RFP spans three calls: I. Local Compute, II. Coordination and accountability, and III. AI-first science (Bio, Neuro, Nano). Grants typically range from $30,000 to $100,000 (larger amounts possible), with strong priority given to applicants who will be active, in-person contributors at the Nodes; review takes roughly three months after the deadline.
October 31, 2026, 23:59 PDT
Coefficient Giving — Project Tailwind
Project Tailwind is Coefficient Giving's call for founders to launch ambitious new AI safety organisations, funded across three tiers: pre-seed $200K–$2M, seed $2M–$20M, and scale $20M–$200M, typically covering 18–36 months of runway. Applicants can pitch their own idea or pick from Coefficient's published list of unsolved problems (e.g. training fault injections, AI decision-making tools, alignment moonshots, a frontier capabilities observatory). Pre-seed applications do not require an existing legal entity, and non-US teams and public-benefit for-profits are eligible.
Rolling
Falcon Fund (Manifund)
The Falcon Fund is a Manifund-hosted animal welfare fund led by regrantor Marcus Abramovitch, making rapid (under 1 week) early-stage grants of $25K–$150K, with a particular focus on the intersection of animal welfare and transformative AI. Priority areas include animal harm benchmarks, animal welfare constitutions for frontier models, industry watchdog work, and raising animal welfare salience inside AI labs. The fund has raised $379,932 toward a $2M goal and grantmaking is largely active/outbound, though contact is open via marcus.s.abramovitch@gmail.com.
Rolling
Manifund Regranting
Manifund's AI Safety Regranting program gives expert regrantors independent budgets of $100K–$350K to fund AI safety projects they identify through their networks or via Manifund's open call. Because a single regrantor owns each budget rather than a committee, grants can be approved in days rather than months, and example grants have ranged from $11K to $200K. Manifund's 501c3 fiscal sponsorship lets regrantors fund individuals, unregistered projects, and (pending diligence) for-profit organizations, with all grants published publicly.
Rolling
Paradigm 3 Research Fund
Paradigm 3's research fund supports work on measuring and forecasting AI capabilities and impacts, alongside its in-house research. Current grantees are studying AI externalities (e.g. time lost to authentication challenges), decomposing benchmark gains into cheating/memorization/shallow vs. OOD generalization, and the desirability and mechanics of slowing AI progress. They invite proposals on topics including centaur evals, 'guardian angel' user-aligned models, the virtue-vs-rules alignment divide, generalization gaps, scaling and 'unhobbling', automated science, compute growth, and recursive self-improvement — prospective grantees email hi@paradigm3.org.
Rolling
Thinking Machines Lab — Safety Research Grants
Thinking Machines Lab is offering Tinker grants of up to $50,000 in API/compute credits for safety research on open-weight models, following the release of their Inkling and Inkling-Small open-weight models. Priority research directions include making models safer to open (differential defense/offense capability, hazardous-data classifiers, tamper-resistant safety training), understanding alignment failure modes (emergent misalignment, reward hacking and oversight gaming), and measuring and forecasting risk (worst-case and marginal risk estimation). The list of directions is explicitly non-exhaustive and any safety project that could be accelerated by additional Tinker credits is welcome.

Astra is Constellation's flagship 5-month, fully funded, in-person fellowship at its Berkeley research center, pairing emerging AI safety talent with senior mentors across an Empirical Research stream and a Strategy & Governance stream. Fellows receive a $8,400/month stipend, up to ~$15K/month in compute budget for empirical researchers, visa support, workspace, and dedicated research management and career support. Over 80% of the first cohort now work full-time in AI safety at organizations such as Redwood Research, METR, Anthropic, OpenAI, Google DeepMind, CAISI, and the UK AI Security Institute.

DeepMind Student Researcher
Google DeepMind's Student Researcher Program is a paid in-person internship placing students enrolled in Bachelor's, Master's or PhD programs alongside leading AI researchers across Google DeepMind, Google Research and other Google AI teams. Placements run 12–24 weeks with a minimum commitment of four days per week at a Google office. Applications are organized by degree level (BS, MS, PhD) and location, and a single application is considered for all relevant positions across Google's AI teams.

LASR Labs — Summer 2026
LASR Labs is a 13-week technical AI safety research programme in London where participants work in teams of 3-4 with an experienced supervisor to take a project from proposal to publication. Participants receive a £15,000 stipend plus food, office space, and travel, working in person from the London Initiative for Safe AI. Alumni have gone on to UK AISI, Apollo Research, OpenAI, Geodesic Research and Coefficient Giving, and LASR papers have been accepted at NeurIPS, ICLR and ICML — including three NeurIPS 2025 main conference papers and an ICML 2025 workshop best paper award.

OpenAI Emerging Talent
OpenAI's Emerging Talent hub gathers early-career pathways into the company: internships, a research residency, and full-time roles in research, applied engineering, and product for people with 0–3 years of experience. It's aimed at recent graduates and self-taught learners who want to work on building safe, beneficial AI, with openings listed continuously on the OpenAI careers board. Prospective applicants can also join the Emerging Talent Community for networking, virtual Q&As, and community events, and consult the OpenAI Interview Guide.
