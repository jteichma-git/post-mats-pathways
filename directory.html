What AI Safety Orgs Want in a Hire

Survey results from 38 AI safety organization leaders published September 2025 revealing what employers look for in candidates. Key findings: 1-3 years of relevant experience is often sufficient for many roles (excluding technical AI research); top upskilling programs include BlueDot courses, Talos Fellowship, and MATS; hardest-to-fill profiles include chiefs of staff, policy professionals, Mandarin speakers, and European citizens with AI context. A free, self-paced introductory course on AGI safety from Google DeepMind's safety research team, consisting of ~75 minutes of short recorded talks and exercises with an accompanying slide deck and exercise workbook. Covers the case for advanced AI capabilities, risks from misaligned goals (instrumental subgoals and deliberate planning), how misalignment arises through specification gaming and goal misgeneralization, and DeepMind's current technical and governance approaches including amplified oversight, robust training, security & control, interpretability, and frontier safety practices. Outsider's Roadmap to AI Safety (LessWrong)

A comprehensive guide published in July 2025 for professionals transitioning into AI safety research from technical backgrounds such as software engineering, physics, or data science. Covers three critical domains: interpretability research (reverse-engineering AI systems to understand their inner workings), alignment research (ensuring AI systems remain beneficial and share human values), and AI control & governance (technical policy for advanced systems). Written by someone actively making the transition, synthesizing insights from Neel Nanda's mechanistic interpretability resources, 80,000 Hours career research, and the broader alignment community, with practical advice on evolving roles (the shift from separate research engineers/scientists toward 'full-stack researchers'), required skills, and pathways for newcomers to contribute meaningfully to the field.

8. Policy & Governance Internships

Fellowships and internships at policy orgs, think tanks, and government-adjacent institutions working on AI governance and safety policy.

IAPS AI Policy Fellowship

The IAPS AI Policy Fellowship is a fully-funded, 3-month program for professionals from varied backgrounds seeking to strengthen practical policy skills for AI governance. Fellows receive $18K (Fellows) or $24K (Senior Fellows) stipends, with an additional $3K for those on the DC or London track, plus travel and accommodation support. The program includes a required 2-week in-person residency in Washington D.C. (February 22 - March 5, 2027), with options to complete remaining time in D.C., London, or remotely.
September 27, 2026

Americans for Responsible Innovation

Americans for Responsible Innovation (ARI) is a bipartisan AI policy nonprofit based in Washington, DC that advocates for thoughtful and proactive AI policy frameworks. They offer a referral bonus program of $3,000 for Director/VP level positions and $1,500 for other roles. The careers page currently shows no open positions listed.

CNAS Nye Internship

The Joseph S. Nye, Jr. National Security Internship and Mentoring Program at CNAS offers rigorous policy research internships across multiple programs including Communications, Design, Development, Defense, Energy Economics and Security, and Indo-Pacific Security. Interns work full-time for six months with a $1,900/month stipend plus $75/month transportation allowance. The program is in-person in Washington, DC and provides direct mentorship from CNAS staff on national security policy issues including defense innovation, economic statecraft, and regional security.

CSET Summer Internship (Georgetown)

A 10-week summer internship at Georgetown's Center for Security and Emerging Technology focused on emerging technologies (AI, cybersecurity, biotechnology) and national security policy. Designed specifically for students from Historically Black Colleges and Universities (HBCUs) and other Minority-Serving Institutions (MSIs). Interns receive a competitive hourly wage plus living stipend, housing at Georgetown University housing in downtown DC (55 H St NW), and travel reimbursement.

RAND CAST Fellows

The RAND Center on AI, Security, and Technology Fellowship develops policy analysts and implementors at the intersection of technology and security. Fellows perform research in AI Security, Technology Policy and Governance, or Biosecurity, with $40,000–$200,000/year stipends for full-time fellows plus monthly health/dental payments. Fellowships run 6 months to 3 years, can be full- or part-time, and are based in Washington DC, San Francisco, Pittsburgh, remote US locations, or the UK.
Applications reviewed quarterly: February 1st (response by March 1st), May 1st (response by June 1st), August 1st (response by September 1st), November 1st (response by December 1st)

DeepMind Student Researcher

Paid research internship program for students enrolled in Bachelor's, Master's, or PhD programs to collaborate with leading AI researchers at Google DeepMind, Google Research, and other Google AI teams. Placements run between 12 and 24 weeks with a minimum commitment of four days per week, working in-person at a Google office. Applications are organized by degree level (BS, MS, PhD) and location, with placement across various Google AI teams based on skills and interests.
