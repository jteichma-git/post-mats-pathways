1. Career Resources

Technical safety & governance roles at labs and orgs. The AI safety roles significantly outpaced general tech hiring in 2024–25. Internships are a strong entry point.

80,000 Hours Job Board

The 80,000 Hours Job Board is a continuously updated, handpicked listing of high-impact roles addressing pressing global problems, including AI safety, AI policy, and existential risk reduction. Users can search and filter by salary (with annualised and USD-converted figures), location, and role type, exclude career-development roles, save or hide listings, view personalised matches based on their background, and subscribe to daily or weekly job alerts. Free 1-1 career advice and extensive research — the career guide, AI careers, list of top skills, and list of world problems — sit alongside the board.
Updated continuously

80K Hours AI Safety Career Review

A comprehensive 80,000 Hours career review by Benjamin Hilton (last updated August 2024, first published June 2023) on AI safety technical research, covering two main paths: empirical AI safety — open to strong software engineers who can apply for research contributor roles immediately, even without an ML background — and theoretical AI safety, suited to those with very strong maths/theoretical CS backgrounds or the ability to get into a top 10 ML PhD program. It covers why the work is high impact, downsides and risk of harm, earnings, predicting personal fit, learning resources, whether to do a PhD, job application strategies, and key organisations. This is a permanent resource page with no application deadline, linked to 80,000 Hours' job board, free AI career advisor chatbot, and free 1-on-1 advising.

AISafety.com Jobs

AISafety.com Jobs is a continuously updated job board currently listing 535 open roles across AI safety research, policy, software engineering, operations, communications and security. Recent postings include Google DeepMind, the UK AI Security Institute, RAND Corporation, Apollo Research, Palisade Research, Encode, The New York Times, the EU Joint Research Centre and the AI Alignment Foundation, with advertised pay ranging from roughly $50/hour to $400,000 (plus UK roles at £65,000–£145,000 and EU roles at €4,449–€6,444/month). Roles span junior to senior leadership, many remote or based in the San Francisco Bay Area, London, Washington DC and elsewhere, and many don't require technical skills.

Verified Mechanisms

Verified Mechanisms is an independent, grant-funded research project (via Manifund) building a formally verified autoresearch pipeline for theoretical mechanistic interpretability, where multiple frontier LLM agents collaborate to generate theorems that are machine-checked in Lean 4. The pilot problem asks how many attention heads are required to compute a Boolean function, with outputs including an open-source multi-agent orchestration harness and the xorformer theorem ledger repository. The team of six researchers says it plans to onboard additional researchers over the coming months for multi-agent orchestration and mech interp work — there is no formal application form, but interested people can join the Discord or email hello@verifiedmechanisms.ai.
Rolling

What AI Safety Orgs Want in a Hire

Blog post from 80,000 Hours (published September 23rd, 2025) by Conor Barnes sharing survey results from 38 leaders of top AI safety organisations about their hiring needs. Key takeaways: 1–3 years of relevant experience is often enough for many non-technical-research and policy roles, and the most-recommended upskilling routes are BlueDot courses, the Talos Fellowship, MATS, GovAI fellowships, the Cambridge ERA:AI Fellowship, and Pivotal Research. It stresses publishing your work publicly, networking at events like EA Global, notes hardest-to-fill profiles (chiefs of staff, policy experience, Mandarin speakers, European citizens with AI context), and points to the 80,000 Hours job board with 300+ AI safety roles.

ALIGN — AI Alignment Network

ALIGN (AI Alignment Network), a Japanese non-profit general incorporated association founded in September 2023, is recruiting volunteer staff for event operations, community management, PR/social media, research and writing, website maintenance, and organizational administration. Applicants should ideally commit 3–4+ hours per week and have basic admin/IT skills; English ability is welcomed, and depending on fit and ALIGN's finances a paid role of roughly 1–2 days per week may be discussed. Apply via the Contact form with name, affiliation, experience (100–400 characters), what you'd like to do at ALIGN (~400 characters), and availability, followed by document screening and an online interview.
Rolling

BlueDot Impact

BlueDot Impact is a non-profit AI safety talent accelerator with staff in San Francisco, London, and remote, offering free online courses, grants, and intensive in-person programs. Since 2022 it has trained 10,000+ professionals, and 25% of graduates land impactful roles within six months at organisations like Anthropic, Google DeepMind, and the UK AI Security Institute. The entry point is the free, self-paced 2-hour "The Future of AI" course with no application required, followed by longer courses, funding, in-person programs, and weekly global events.
Multiple cohorts per year

Google DeepMind: AGI Safety Short Course

A free, self-paced introductory course on AGI safety from Google DeepMind's safety research team, consisting of ~75 minutes of short recorded talks and exercises plus an accompanying slide deck and exercise workbook. It covers the path to superhuman capabilities, risks from deliberate planning and instrumental subgoals, how misaligned goals arise (specification gaming vs. goal misgeneralization), DeepMind's technical alignment approach (amplified oversight, robust training & monitoring, security & control, interpretability, safer design patterns, alignment stress tests), and its governance approach including frontier safety practices and dangerous capability evaluations. Aimed at students, researchers and professionals who want an accessible one-hour introduction to AI alignment.
Self-paced — no deadline

MIT AI Risk Initiative

The MIT AI Risk Initiative is an ongoing research project producing open data and frameworks for identifying, prioritizing, and managing AI risks. Its tools include the AI Risk Repository (a living taxonomy, now at Version 4), an AI Incident Tracker, a governance database mapping global AI laws and policies to risk domains, an AI Risk Mitigation Database, an expert survey on priority risks, and the AI Risk Navigator, an interactive tool connecting all datasets via shared taxonomies. The site also publishes a blog with research updates, framework summaries, and project news, plus a newsletter to join.

ML4Good Technical Bootcamp

ML4Good's Technical Programme is a free, residential AI safety bootcamp covering transformers from scratch, LLM agents, interpretability, RLHF, evaluations, technical governance, and a 2-day capstone project with mentorship and 1-on-1 career sessions. Food, accommodation and teaching are provided at no cost, travel may be reimbursed, and cohorts are organised regionally so participants come from the same area; all sessions run in English. It targets people with some coding experience (coding is ~25-30% of the curriculum) who are committed to contributing substantially to AI safety — alumni have gone on to MATS, SaferAI, UK AISI and the EU AI Office.
Not yet announced — join notification list

Outsider's Roadmap to AI Safety (LessWrong)

A comprehensive guide published in July 2025 for professionals transitioning into AI safety research from technical backgrounds such as software engineering, physics, or data science. Covers three critical domains: interpretability research (reverse-engineering AI systems to understand their inner workings), alignment research (ensuring AI systems remain beneficial and share human values), and AI control & governance (technical policy for advanced systems). Written by someone actively making the transition, synthesizing insights from Neel Nanda's mechanistic interpretability resources, 80,000 Hours career research, and the broader alignment community, with practical advice on evolving roles (the shift from separate research engineers/scientists toward 'full-stack researchers'), required skills, and pathways for newcomers to contribute meaningfully to the field.

Successif

Successif provides free, expert-guided career development for experienced professionals moving into AI risk reduction work. Support includes personalized 1:1 advising with dedicated advisors, resume/application feedback and mock interviews, expert-led workshops and self-paced courses, direct introductions to AI risk organizations with early access to jobs, and a peer community via Discord and events. Specialized tracks include an AI Policy Strategy Fellowship and a Women's Program for underrepresented groups.

3. Fellowships & Research Programs

AI safety fellowships with stipends, mentorship, and compute. Many programs run multiple cohorts per year across the US and UK.

AE Studio — AI Alignment Research Grants

The AI Alignment Foundation (formerly AE Studio's alignment arm) provides research funding plus dedicated engineering teams to accelerate high-risk, high-reward alignment research beyond mechanistic interpretability, evals, and control. It targets alignment researchers with ideas their current institutional funding won't cover, as well as technical experts from other fields (cognitive neuroscience, mathematics, physics) whose perspectives could open new directions. Engineering teams can execute proposals with as little as one hour per week of the researcher's time, and applicants describe a first experiment a good engineering team could run in 1–2 weeks.
Rolling

CBAI Summer Research Fellowship

The Cambridge Boston Alignment Initiative (CBAI) Research Fellowship is an intensive, fully-funded ten-week program in Cambridge, Massachusetts for researchers building careers in technical AI safety, AI governance, or technical governance. Fellows receive an $8,000 stipend, housing for non-local participants, weekday meals, 24/7 Harvard Square workspace, up to $10,000 in compute credits, and 1–2 hours weekly of mentorship from researchers at Harvard, MIT, Google DeepMind, the UK AI Security Institute, CNAS, RAND, and the Institute for Progress. Open to undergraduates, master's and PhD students, postdocs, and early-career professionals (18+; OPT/CPT accepted but no visa sponsorship), with an extension program of up to 4 months available.
Rolling — Expression of Interest open for Spring 2026

Dovetail Research Fellowship

Dovetail is a research group pursuing foundational mathematics research aimed at understanding the nature of AI agents, with the mission of helping humanity safely navigate the creation of powerful AI systems. It is funded in part by the Long-Term Future Fund (LTFF) and the Advanced Research + Invention Agency (ARIA), and its research agenda is published as a public wiki. The landing page links to a Jobs section but lists no fellowship cohort dates, stipend, or application details.

Frontier AI Security Residency

The Frontier AI Security Residency (FASR) is an 8-week, fully funded in-person research and engineering programme in Cambridge, UK, run by ERA with Heron and the Oxford Hardware AI Governance Lab. Residents work alongside mentors from leading AI hardware and cybersecurity teams on concrete projects in hardware-enabled verification, cybersecurity and policy, running 12 Oct – 4 Dec 2026. No formal application form is live — the site currently collects interest through its mailing list, with talks, poster sessions at ERA conferences, and a live-in cohort community.
Not yet announced — mailing list open for the 12 Oct – 4 Dec 2026 cohort

Pivotal Research Fellowship

A 15-week full-time, in-person AI safety research fellowship in London (18 January – 30 April 2027) hosted at the London Initiative for Safe AI (LISA), with weekly 1-on-1 mentorship from established researchers, dedicated research managers, workshops, and speaker sessions. Fellows receive a £6,000–£8,000 stipend (Senior Fellows with a PhD or strong track record get £8,000), plus travel, £2,400 housing support for non-London fellows, weekday meals, and compute. Open to anyone 18 or older committed to making AI go well regardless of academic background; the process involves a written application, video interview, mentor work task, and final interview.

The site hosts a Replications Fellowship alongside published replication work, a team page, and forms to give feedback or request a specific replication. No application window, stipend, duration, or location details are listed on the landing page.

4. Grants & Funding

Fund your own research through grants, regranters, and open RFPs. Many programs offer rolling applications with fast turnaround.

Foresight Institute AI Nodes

Foresight Institute's AI for Science & Safety Nodes RFP offers grant funding, office space, and access to private local compute clusters at physical hubs in San Francisco and Berlin, plus a community xNode in Cape Town with the Cape Institute for Safe AI (CISAI). The call spans three areas: I. Local Compute, II. Coordination and accountability, and III. AI-first science (Bio, Neuro, Nano), with grants typically $30,000–$100,000 (larger amounts possible). Individuals, teams, non-profits and for-profits may apply; strong priority goes to applicants who will be active, in-person contributors at the Nodes, and review takes roughly three months after the deadline.
October 31, 2026, 23:59 PDT

Coefficient Giving — Project Tailwind

Project Tailwind is Coefficient Giving's call for founders to launch ambitious new AI safety organisations, funded across three tiers: pre-seed $200K–$2M, seed $2M–$20M, and scale $20M–$200M, typically covering 18–36 months of runway. Applicants can pitch their own idea or take on one of Coefficient's published unsolved problems in AI safety. Pre-seed applicants do not need an existing legal entity, and non-US teams and public-benefit for-profits are eligible.
Rolling

Falcon Fund (Manifund)

The Falcon Fund is a Manifund-hosted animal welfare fund led by regrantor Marcus Abramovitch, making rapid (under 1 week) early-stage grants of $25K–$150K, with a particular focus on the intersection of animal welfare and transformative AI. Priority areas include animal harm benchmarks, animal welfare constitutions for frontier models, industry watchdog work on AI in factory farming, and raising animal welfare salience inside AI labs. Launched April 2026, it has raised $379,932 toward a $2M goal, with all grants published publicly; grantmaking is largely active/outbound, though contact is open via marcus.s.abramovitch@gmail.com.
Rolling

Manifund Regranting

Manifund's AI Safety Regranting program gives expert regrantors independent budgets of $100K–$350K to fund AI safety projects sourced from their own networks or from Manifund's open call. Because a single regrantor owns each budget instead of a committee, grants can be approved in days rather than months, with example regrants ranging from $11K to $200K. Manifund's 501c3 fiscal sponsorship lets regrantors fund individuals, unregistered projects, and (pending diligence) for-profits, and all grants — including the regrantor's written rationale — are published publicly.
Rolling

Paradigm 3 Research Fund

Paradigm 3's research fund supports external work on measuring and forecasting AI capabilities and impacts, alongside its in-house research. Current grantees are estimating the externalities of today's AI systems (starting with time lost to authentication challenges), decomposing benchmark gains into cheating, memorization, shallow generalization and OOD generalization, and studying when and how slowing AI progress would be desirable. They invite proposals on topics including centaur evals, 'guardian angel' user-aligned models, the virtue-vs-rules alignment divide, generalization gaps, scaling and 'unhobbling', automated science, compute growth, job displacement, specialized vs. generalist AI, and recursive self-improvement — prospective grantees email hi@paradigm3.org.
Rolling

Thinking Machines Lab — Safety Research Grants

Thinking Machines Lab is offering Tinker grants of up to $50,000 in API/compute credits for safety research on open-weight models, launched alongside their Inkling and Inkling-Small open-weight releases. Priority directions include making models safer to open (differential defense-vs-offense capability uplift, large-scale hazardous-data classifiers, tamper-resistant safety training), understanding alignment failure modes (emergent misalignment from narrow fine-tuning, reward hacking and oversight/chain-of-thought gaming), and measuring and forecasting risk (worst-case and marginal risk estimation under adversarial fine-tuning). The list is explicitly non-exhaustive — any safety project that could be accelerated by additional Tinker credits is welcome.

5. Incubators & Startup Programs

Launch a new org or project via incubators and grants. Multiple programs offer stipends, mentorship, co-founder matching, and seed funding.

Allelo

Allelo is a new AI safety research initiative (fiscally sponsored by Rethink Priorities) studying what propensities and character traits make AI agents safe to deploy in complex, multi-agent, sometimes adversarial real-world settings, with an initial focus on susceptibility to influence. It aims to produce concrete agent-design recommendations for frontier labs, regulators, and certification bodies. The team is hiring a full-time Senior Researcher at USD 120,000–150,000 (higher possible for exceptional candidates), location-flexible with London preferred, and potentially convertible to a co-founder role.
Rolling

BlueDot Impact — Incubator Week

BlueDot Impact's Incubator Week is a 5-day, all-expenses-paid founder sprint in San Francisco for technically serious people in AI safety, biosecurity, cyber, or other catastrophic-risk fields: build threat models Monday, test interventions Tuesday–Wednesday, prep Thursday, and pitch Friday for up to $100k in grant funding (delivered within two weeks if you make good progress). Past cohorts total 50 participants who have raised $7M+ externally, with alumni including Exona Lab, 0Labs, Telluvian, Alma Grants, and safely.bio, plus career-transition grants and placements at Andon Labs, ASET, and BlueDot. No polished idea required — you just need to be ready to leave your current role and build; referrals earn $2,000 per accepted person.
Cohort v6 runs November 2–6, 2026 — applications rolling

Catalyze Impact

Catalyze Impact is a 501(c)(3) non-profit incubator for AI safety, security, and resilience organizations, offering highly customized 1-on-1 support on direction & strategy, co-founder matching, and mentorship from an expert network of industry leaders and experienced entrepreneurs. Founders access a seed funding network of grantmakers and investors plus a stipend to work full-time, and join private office hours with figures such as Jaime Sevilla (Epoch AI), Chris Akin (Apollo Research), and Eric Ho (Goodfire). The program has incubated ~15 organizations to date, with the majority raising 6 or 7 figures, spanning AI control, governance, and hardware security.
Rolling — get in touch

d_model

d_model is a fundamental AI research lab partnering with frontier labs to turn their models into capable interpretability and alignment researchers, building RL environments for open-ended interpretability tasks that teach agents to do cutting-edge research rather than reward-hack. The lab also pursues independent interpretability research, with publications on LLM-agent-discovered concept-erasure algorithms, how models represent program nullability, and steering characters via interpretability. Career interest is collected through a general Hiring Interest Form rather than a formal application window.
Rolling

Halcyon Futures

Halcyon Futures is a nonprofit incubator and venture fund backing exceptional founders, operators, and researchers building in AI safety and alignment, biosecurity, AI/cybersecurity, and governance and policy. It offers career transition grants up to $500K, nonprofit seed grants up to $1M (often wired within days), and venture investments of $1M+, plus incubation, co-founder matching, introductions to frontier labs, VCs and funders, and multi-day founder retreats. Its portfolio spans 25+ launched organizations that have raised $400M+ in follow-on funding, alongside 150 supported career transitions.
Rolling

ImpactOps

Impact Ops is an operations consultancy providing consultancy and hands-on specialist support to high-impact organizations across AI safety, global health and development, animal welfare, and climate change. Services span finance (budgets to bookkeeping), recruitment, entity setup and fiscal-sponsor spin-outs, donor/grantee due diligence, operational audits, and systems implementation. Clients include the Centre for Long-Term Resilience, BlueDot Impact, Foresight Institute, Forecasting Research Institute, Longview Philanthropy, Giving What We Can, Condor Initiative, Impact Academy, and Arkose, with a Careers page and contact form for engaging services.

Neolithic

Neolithic is a newly founded nonprofit startup in San Francisco building open-source, agentic tools and infrastructure to accelerate AI safety research, with projects spanning rogue-deployment datasets, evaluation-awareness mitigation, agent infrastructure for research, and automated reward hacking detection. It is funded to bring on its first two hires immediately, offering end-to-end project ownership and direct collaboration with researchers at leading AI safety organizations. It also invites safety researchers and orgs to request custom tooling, which Neolithic will build and maintain for free.
Rolling

RESI — Institute for Responsible Superintelligence

RESI (Institute for Responsible Superintelligence) is a nonprofit research institute in Cambridge, Massachusetts building the scientific foundations for superintelligence that is "safe by design," applying the cryptography playbook of precise definitions, constructions, composition, and impossibility results to AI safety. It was founded by Turing Award winner Shafi Goldwasser, cryptographer Vinod Vaikuntanathan, and former OpenAI Safety Systems researcher Adam Tauman Kalai, with participating researchers spanning computer science, mathematics, economics, and law. Research is AI-assisted from the start and organized into 11 named working groups — including formal verification (Adam Chlipala), cryptographic verification (Goldwasser), monitoring and control (Vaikuntanathan), modular architectures (Kalai), game-theoretic foundations (Yannai A. Gonczarowski), ethics (Chloé Bakalar), legal alignment (Noam Kolt), AI harnesses, and data attribution — alongside an active visitor program.

Rethink Priorities Special Projects

Rethink Priorities' Special Projects Program provides fiscal sponsorship and end-to-end operational support — tax-exempt grant receipt, finance and accounting, recruitment and hiring through US/UK entities or an international Employer of Record, visa facilitation, fundraising coordination, and event planning — so high-impact initiatives can launch and scale quickly. Fees for new projects are typically around 14% of project costs, varying with needs, complexity, and scale. Through the end of 2025 the team had received 195 expressions of interest, supported 15 projects, expended $19.7M in direct costs, onboarded 114 sponsee staff, completed 16 hiring rounds (three ongoing), 32 successful US/UK visa applications, and 20 in-person events with 500 attendees.
Rolling

Seldon Lab

Seldon Lab is an accelerator and venture studio investing in and hands-on building with founders working exclusively on world-scale AGI deployment and existential security technologies. Beyond capital and mentorship, the team publishes papers, builds systems, and deploys cyber-physical infrastructure, with network guests including Geoff Ralston, Emmett Shear, and Josh Achiam, and a team including Eric Ries, Esben Kran, Finn Metz, and Nick Fitz. Batch #1 alumni include Lucid Computing and Andon Labs; Batch #2's Demo Day was held Apr 20, 2026, and applications are accepted on a rolling basis via the site's Apply Now page.

The Alignment Journal is now open to submissions from everyone. I’m part of the team behind it.

It’s a free-to-read, free-to-publish journal for research on the scientific foundations of understanding, predicting and steering AI systems. It welcomes work that builds theoretical or otherwise principled analyses, and whose significance survives the deletion of any particular model, dataset or benchmark. Empirical investigations of such ideas are welcome, and negative results are encouraged. Of particular interest are approaches that could apply to future superintelligent systems that interact and self-improve.

What the Journal does differently:

  • Every accepted paper is published with a “review summary” to retain insights generated during the review.
  • Reviewers are paid, with bonuses for speed and insight.
  • Each paper is judged against the Journal’s acceptance bar, not against other submissions.
  • Papers under review are listed publicly, so researchers can nominate themselves to review one.

If you have work that falls within the Journal’s scope, consider submitting. If you want to review or act as an action editor, create an account for that at our website.
