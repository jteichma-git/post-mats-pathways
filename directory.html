1. Career Resources

Technical safety & governance roles at labs and orgs. The AI safety roles significantly outpaced general tech hiring in 2024–25. Internships are a strong entry point.

80,000 Hours Job Board

The 80,000 Hours Job Board is a continuously updated, handpicked listing of high-impact roles tackling pressing global problems, with a strong emphasis on AI safety, AI policy, and existential risk reduction. Users can search and filter by annualised, USD-converted salary, location and role type, exclude career-development roles, save or hide listings, see personalised matches based on their background, and subscribe to daily or weekly job alerts. It sits alongside free 1-1 career advice, an AI advisor chat, and 80,000 Hours' research including the career guide, AI careers, top skills and list of world problems.
Updated continuously

80K Hours AI Safety Career Review

A comprehensive 80,000 Hours career review by Benjamin Hilton (last updated August 2024, first published June 2023) on AI safety technical research, covering two main paths: empirical AI safety — open to strong software engineers who can apply for research contributor roles immediately, even without an ML background — and theoretical AI safety, suited to those with very strong maths or theoretical CS backgrounds, or who could get into a top 10 ML PhD program. It reviews why the work is high impact, the downsides and risk of doing harm, earnings, how to predict personal fit, learning resources, whether to do a PhD, job-hunting strategies, and key organisations (including teams at Google DeepMind, OpenAI, MIT, Cambridge, CMU and UC Berkeley). The review notes the field is highly neglected — an estimated ~300 people worked on technical approaches to AI existential risk as of 2022 — and links to 80,000 Hours' job board, AI career advisor and free 1-on-1 advising.

AISafety.com Jobs

AISafety.com Jobs is a continuously updated job board now listing 575 open roles (plus 5 more) spanning AI safety research, policy, software engineering, operations, communications and security. Recent listings include MIT FutureTech, Anthropic, Google DeepMind, FAR AI, Encode, the AI Alignment Foundation, Constellation, Pour Demain, Global Shield and the National Research Council Canada, with advertised pay from around $90,000 to $450,000 (plus UK roles up to £640,000, CAD 119,688–168,192, CHF 105,000–125,000 and €65,000–€80,000). Roles range from entry-level to chief-officer leadership, many remote or based in the San Francisco Bay Area, London, Washington DC, Ottawa and across Europe, and many don't require technical skills.
Updated continuously

Verified Mechanisms

Verified Mechanisms is an independent, grant-funded (via Manifund) research project building a formally verified autoresearch pipeline for theoretical mechanistic interpretability, where multiple frontier LLM agents collaborate to generate theorems that are machine-checked in Lean 4. The pilot question asks how many attention heads are required to compute a Boolean function, with outputs including an open-source multi-agent orchestration harness, the xorformer theorem ledger repo, and an oral paper at the AutoMLR Workshop at NeurIPS 2026. There is no formal application form — the team of research leads, interpretability researchers, engineers and Talmenta mentees invites interested people to join the Discord or email hello@verifiedmechanisms.ai.
Rolling

What AI Safety Orgs Want in a Hire

Blog post from 80,000 Hours (published September 23, 2025) by Conor Barnes summarising a survey of 38 leaders of top AI safety organisations about their hiring needs. Key findings: 1–3 years of relevant experience is often enough for non-technical-research and policy roles, and the most-recommended upskilling routes are BlueDot courses, the Talos Fellowship, MATS, GovAI fellowships, the Cambridge ERA:AI Fellowship, and Pivotal Research. It stresses publishing work publicly and networking at events like EA Global, notes the hardest-to-fill profiles (chiefs of staff, policy experience, Mandarin speakers, European citizens with AI context), and links to the 80,000 Hours job board with 300+ AI safety roles.

ALIGN (AI Alignment Network), a Japanese non-profit general incorporated association founded in September 2023, is recruiting volunteer staff for event operations, community management, PR/social media, research and writing, website maintenance, and organizational administration. Google DeepMind: AGI Safety Short Course

A free, self-paced introductory course on AGI safety from Google DeepMind's safety research team, comprising ~75 minutes of short recorded talks and exercises plus a slide deck and exercise workbook. It covers the path to superhuman capabilities, risks from deliberate planning and instrumental subgoals, how misaligned goals arise (specification gaming vs. goal misgeneralization), DeepMind's technical alignment approach (amplified oversight, robust training & monitoring, security & control, interpretability, safer design patterns, alignment stress tests), and its governance approach including frontier safety practices and dangerous capability evaluations. Aimed at students, researchers and professionals who want an accessible one-hour introduction to AI alignment.
Self-paced — no deadline

MIT AI Risk Initiative

The MIT AI Risk Initiative (at MIT FutureTech) is an ongoing research project producing open, CC BY 4.0-licensed data and frameworks for identifying, prioritizing, and managing AI risks. Its tools include the AI Risk Repository (1,700+ risks from 65 frameworks), an AI Incident Tracker, a global governance database mapping AI laws and policies to risk domains, a mitigations database (2,206 mitigations from 47 frameworks), company disclosure data covering the world's 200 largest firms, an expert survey of 272 international AI experts, a new existential-risk scenario review, and the AI Risk Navigator that connects all datasets via shared taxonomies. There are no applications — the site offers a newsletter, a blog with research updates, and a "get in touch" route for partnerships.

ML4Good Technical Bootcamp

ML4Good's Technical Programme is a free, residential AI safety bootcamp covering transformers built from scratch, LLM agents, interpretability, RLHF, evaluations, technical governance, and a 2-day capstone project with mentorship and 1-on-1 career sessions. Food, accommodation and teaching are provided at no cost, travel may be reimbursed, and cohorts are organised regionally so participants come from the same area; all sessions run in English. It targets people with some coding experience (coding is ~25-30% of the curriculum) who are committed to contributing substantially to AI safety — alumni have gone on to MATS, SaferAI, UK AISI and the EU AI Office, and 98% of participants recommend it.
Not yet announced — join notification list

Outsider's Roadmap to AI Safety (LessWrong)

A comprehensive guide published in July 2025 for professionals transitioning into AI safety research from technical backgrounds such as software engineering, physics, or data science. Covers three critical domains: interpretability research (reverse-engineering AI systems to understand their inner workings), alignment research (ensuring AI systems remain beneficial and share human values), and AI control & governance (technical policy for advanced systems). Written by someone actively making the transition, synthesizing insights from Neel Nanda's mechanistic interpretability resources, 80,000 Hours career research, and the broader alignment community, with practical advice on evolving roles (the shift from separate research engineers/scientists toward 'full-stack researchers'), required skills, and pathways for newcomers to contribute meaningfully to the field.

Successif

Successif offers free, expert-guided career development for experienced professionals transitioning into AI risk reduction work. Support includes a dedicated 1:1 advisor, resume and application feedback, mock interviews, expert-led workshops and self-paced courses on technical, policy and media topics, direct introductions to AI risk organizations with early access to jobs, and a peer community via Discord and social events. Specialized tracks include an AI Policy Strategy Fellowship and a Women's Program for women and other underrepresented groups.

3. Fellowships & Research Programs

AI safety fellowships with stipends, mentorship, and compute. Many programs run multiple cohorts per year across the US and UK.

AI Safety Camp

AI Safety Camp is an online, part-time AI safety research program (12th edition) where participants join one of 32 preselected projects spanning stop/pause AI, policy and governance, agentic risk and oversight, evaluations, mech-interp, and AI safety education. Team members are expected to commit 10 hours/week and attend weekly meetings across the ~3.5-month program, which runs January to April 2027 and ends with an online final-presentations conference. Applicants can apply to specific projects or submit one generic application covering all of them.
November 29, 2026 (team member applications)

Iliad Intensive / Fellowship

Iliad advances AI alignment as a theoretical and experimental science through in-house research, incubated organizations (AIXI Labs, Principia, Stormglass, XOR Labs), the peer-reviewed Alignment Journal, and training programs. The Iliad Intensive is a 4-week taught program in foundational alignment for technically strong researchers, and the Iliad Fellowship is a ~3-month mentored research program in London or the Bay Area for scholars pivoting into mathematical and theoretical AI safety (Fellows must have completed the Intensive). Research areas include learning theory, debate, heuristic arguments, data attribution, agency, and the geometry of neural networks; about half of Fellows receive a six-month research extension.
Dec 16, 2026 (Feb 2027 Fellowship)

SPAR

SPAR (run by Kairos) is a 3-month, flexible remote fellowship pairing aspiring AI safety, policy, biosecurity, communications and fieldbuilding contributors with experienced mentors from Anthropic, Google DeepMind, UK AISI, Stanford, FAR.AI and more. Fellows commit 5–40 hours/week alongside other work, produce output that has reached ICML, ICLR, NeurIPS and TIME coverage, and present at a virtual Demo Day with $7,000 in prizes plus a career fair with METR, Redwood Research and GovAI. Open to any experience level, from undergraduates to experienced professionals; an expression-of-interest form is open ahead of the Spring 2027 round, which newly includes non-research projects.
Spring 2027 applications open December 16, 2026 and close January 13, 2027 (mentor/org applications close November 30, 2026)

AE Studio — AI Alignment Research Grants

The AI Alignment Foundation (the alignment arm spun out of AE Studio) provides research funding plus dedicated engineering teams to pursue high-risk, high-reward alignment directions beyond mechanistic interpretability, evals, and control. It targets alignment researchers with ideas their institutional funding won't cover, plus technical experts from other fields (cognitive neuroscience, mathematics, physics) whose perspectives could open new directions. Engineering teams can execute proposals with as little as one hour per week of the researcher's time, and applicants are asked to describe a first experiment a good engineering team could run in 1–2 weeks.
Rolling

Anthropic Fellows Program

The Anthropic Fellows Program funds and mentors engineers and researchers tackling Anthropic's highest-priority AI safety questions — scalable oversight, adversarial robustness and AI control, model organisms, mechanistic interpretability, AI security, and model welfare. Fellows spend 4 months on empirical research with an Anthropic mentor, receiving ~$15K/month in compute funding plus a stipend (see the job description for current rates). No PhD, prior ML experience, or published papers required; more than 200 fellows have participated since early 2025, with over 65% publishing their work and over 35% joining Anthropic full-time.
Rolling (January 2027 cohort deadline of October 18, 2026 has passed)

CBAI Summer Research Fellowship

The Cambridge Boston Alignment Initiative (CBAI) Research Fellowship is an intensive, fully-funded ten-week program in Cambridge, Massachusetts for researchers building careers in technical AI safety, AI governance, or technical governance. Fellows receive an $8,000 stipend, housing for non-local participants, weekday meals, 24/7 Harvard Square workspace, up to $10,000 in compute credits, and 1–2 hours weekly of mentorship from researchers at Harvard, MIT, Google DeepMind, the UK AI Security Institute, CNAS, RAND, and the Institute for Progress. Open to undergraduates, master's and PhD students, postdocs, and early-career professionals (18+; OPT/CPT accepted but no visa sponsorship), with an extension program of up to 4 months available.
Rolling — Expression of Interest open for Spring 2026

Dovetail Research Fellowship

Dovetail is a research group pursuing foundational mathematics research aimed at understanding the nature of AI agents, with the mission of helping humanity safely navigate the creation of powerful AI systems. It is funded in part by the Long-Term Future Fund (LTFF) and the Advanced Research + Invention Agency (ARIA), and publishes its research agenda as a public wiki. The landing page links to a Jobs section but lists no fellowship cohort dates, stipend, location, or application details.

4. Grants & Funding

Fund your own research through grants, regranters, and open RFPs. Many programs offer rolling applications with fast turnaround.

Coefficient Giving — Project Tailwind

Project Tailwind is Coefficient Giving's call for founders to launch ambitious new AI safety organisations, funded across three tiers: pre-seed $200K–$2M, seed $2M–$20M, and scale $20M–$200M, typically covering 18–36 months of runway. Applicants can pitch their own idea or take on one of Coefficient's published unsolved problems in AI safety. Pre-seed applicants do not need an existing legal entity, and non-US teams and public-benefit for-profits are eligible.
Rolling

Falcon Fund (Manifund)

The Falcon Fund is a Manifund-hosted animal welfare fund led by regrantor Marcus Abramovitch, making rapid (under 1 week) early-stage grants of $25K–$150K, with a particular focus on the intersection of animal welfare and transformative AI. Priority areas include animal harm benchmarks, animal welfare constitutions for frontier models, watchdog work on AI in factory farming, and raising animal welfare salience inside AI labs. Launched April 2026, it has raised $380,132 from 18 donors toward a $2M goal, with all grants and rationales published publicly; grantmaking is largely active/outbound, though contact is open via marcus.s.abramovitch@gmail.com.
Rolling

Manifund Regranting

Manifund's AI Safety Regranting program gives expert regrantors independent budgets of $100K–$350K to fund AI safety projects sourced from their own networks or from Manifund's open call. Because a single regrantor owns each budget rather than a committee, grants can be approved in days rather than months, with example regrants ranging from $11K to $200K. Manifund's 501c3 fiscal sponsorship lets regrantors fund individuals, unregistered projects, and (pending diligence) for-profits, and every grant — including the regrantor's written rationale — is published publicly.
Rolling

Paradigm 3 Research Fund

Paradigm 3's research fund supports external work on measuring and forecasting AI capabilities and impacts, alongside its in-house research. Current grantees are estimating the externalities of today's AI systems (starting with time lost to authentication challenges), decomposing benchmark gains into cheating, memorization, shallow generalization and OOD generalization, and studying when and how slowing AI progress would be desirable. They invite proposals on centaur evals, 'guardian angel' user-aligned models, the virtue-vs-rules alignment divide, generalization gaps, scaling and 'unhobbling', automated science, compute growth, job displacement, specialized vs. generalist AI, and recursive self-improvement — prospective grantees email hi@paradigm3.org.
Rolling

Thinking Machines Lab — Safety Research Grants

Thinking Machines Lab is offering Tinker grants of up to $50,000 in API/compute credits for safety research on open-weight models, announced alongside their Inkling and Inkling-Small open-weight releases. Priority directions include making models safer to open (differential defense-vs-offense capability uplift, large-scale hazardous-data classifiers, tamper-resistant safety training), understanding alignment failure modes (emergent misalignment from narrow fine-tuning, reward hacking and chain-of-thought oversight gaming), and measuring and forecasting risk (worst-case and marginal risk estimation under adversarial fine-tuning). The list is explicitly non-exhaustive — any safety project that could be accelerated by additional Tinker credits is welcome.
Rolling

5. Incubators & Startup Programs

Launch a new org or project via incubators and grants. Multiple programs offer stipends, mentorship, co-founder matching, and seed funding.

RESI — Institute for Responsible Superintelligence

RESI (Institute for Responsible Superintelligence) is an independent nonprofit AI safety research institute in Cambridge, Massachusetts building the scientific foundations for superintelligence that is "safe by design" — safety properties specified in advance and achieved by mechanisms whose guarantees can be analyzed before deployment, borrowing the modern cryptography playbook of definitions, constructions, and provable guarantees. Tarbell AI Journalism Fellowship

The Tarbell Fellowship is a one-year program for journalists and subject-matter experts covering artificial intelligence, combining a three-month remote AI and journalism fundamentals course, a weeklong in-person summit, and a nine-month newsroom placement at outlets such as CBS News, The Bureau of Investigative Journalism, MIT Technology Review, and TIME. Fellows earn $75,000–$120,000 depending on experience and location, while Senior Fellows (7+ years of journalism or AI experience) earn $115,000–$135,000. 2027-28 partner newsrooms are based in Hong Kong, London, Los Angeles, New York, San Francisco, Seattle, and Washington D.C., so applicants generally need the right to work in the U.S., U.K., or Hong Kong.

AI Security Bootcamp — London

AI Security Bootcamp London is a 7-day intensive, fully funded programme for experienced security professionals working to secure frontier AI systems, running Feb 7–13, 2027 in London. The curriculum spans threat modelling, adversarial attacks, LLM and infrastructure security, weight security and formal methods, data centre security, AI control, and hardware governance, delivered via lectures, demos, guest speakers, and hands-on red/blue team exercises. Tuition, meals, materials, and London accommodation are covered with need-based travel support; 20 participants are accepted and 7+ years of hands-on security experience is required (no prior AI/ML background needed, pre-work provided).

The Alignment Research Center (ARC) is hiring researchers, primarily for visiting researcher positions offered year-round, which typically run ~10 weeks with flexible duration. LASR Labs — Summer 2026

LASR Labs is a 13-week technical AI safety research programme in London where participants work in teams of 3-4 with an experienced supervisor to take a project from proposal to publication. Alumni have gone on to UK AISI, Apollo Research, OpenAI, Geodesic Research and Coefficient Giving, and LASR papers have appeared at NeurIPS, ICLR and ICML — including three NeurIPS 2025 main conference papers (one oral) and an ICML 2025 workshop best paper award.
